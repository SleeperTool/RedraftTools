Playoff Odds

Full simulation of your league: lineups, injuries, bye weeks and live scoring. Enter your Sleeper league ID and press Run.

How each seed is decided, and who gets a bye

Seeds are filled in order: each one goes to the best team not already seeded, by record (points-for breaks ties) or by total points-for.

The first run downloads the Python engine and Sleeper's player list, so it can take 30 seconds or more. Later runs in the same visit are faster. No Sleeper league? Try the quick calculator.

How it works. Each team's strength comes from its roster and its scoring history. Every simulated season plays out the remaining schedule with random weekly scores, sets each team's best lineup, applies injuries and bye weeks, then seeds the teams. The odds are the share of simulated seasons where a team gets in.
Columns. Avg Seed is the team's average seed in the simulated seasons where it gets in. Bye Odds is the chance of landing on any seed you marked for a bye. Record Spot and Points Spot split the playoff odds by how the team got in (shown when you mix both). Playoff Odds is the total chance of getting in.
Injuries. Every simulated season re-checks who is available each week, using Sleeper's injury statuses:
  • Questionable: 78% chance to play.
  • Doubtful: 10% chance to play, then 45% chance each week to be back.
  • Out: misses the next game, then a 75% chance each week to return.
  • IR, PUP and suspended: follow Sleeper's weekly projections, so they sit until projections show them back.
  • New injuries can happen to anyone as the season plays out, a little more often for running backs.
Each week the best legal lineup is rebuilt from whoever is available, and empty spots are filled with the best free agent in your league. The Key injuries panel lists injured players who would start, and Lineup hit is how many points that team's best lineup loses in a week he sits.
Results are estimates. Injury statuses come from Sleeper's player list when you run, and Sleeper only updates records on Tuesday morning.
